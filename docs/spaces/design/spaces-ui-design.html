Spaces in the app: basic setup, full control

How you configure spaces in the macOS app. The goal is a basic setup that's correct in about two minutes for most people, and an advanced editor that exposes every rule without making it easy to weaken isolation by accident. Background in the earlier drafts beside this file: memory-permissions-design.html, github-org-separation-design.html (the space model) and context-isolation-deep-dive.html (enforcement research). The features are SP-050 to SP-055 (the pane) and SP-072 (minimal controls that ship first).

Updated 2026-11-09 to match the plan. The authoritative plan is docs/spaces/spaces-features.json (rendered at docs/spaces/index.html); where they disagree, the plan wins.

Design principles

Only you change policy

The policy lives in orch.db as numbered revisions. Anyone except a queued task may save a draft, and saving changes nothing. Apply is operator-only, names the revision you reviewed, and is refused to anything descended from a harness or orchd. A draft an agent saved shows as pending (drift) and needs your explicit acceptance to apply (D-25, SP-020).

Detect, then confirm

Basic setup proposes spaces from what's already on disk and in GitHub: checkouts grouped by remote org, your org memberships, repo visibility. You confirm. You don't type paths.

Safe things are easy, risky things are visible

Isolated-to-isolated reads aren't a toggle you can flip. They're locked cells that explain why. Combining two jobs is not a setting: the design summary calls it an explicit operator operation, but no feature builds it yet.

Preview every effect

Before Apply, see the policy diff and how many chunks, PRs and tasks move between spaces (counts only, never content). Each space has its own Qdrant collection (D-10). Moving chunks into it runs in the background without re-embedding, and a chunk stays unreadable until it has moved (SP-027, INV-08).

Ask "where would this go?"

A tester takes a path, URL, org/repo or chat channel and shows its space and the rule that decided it. The same check backs orch spaces why and the ExplainPlacement RPC, which exist today (SP-022).

Secrets stay write-only

Term lists can be added to and counted, never displayed. Refusals show a term's line number and hash, not the term (SP-044, INV-07). A one-click screen-share-safe mode also hides every space except one you pick (D-21).

Every layer has an off switch

Memory filtering, the tool guard, outbound terms and sandbox rules each have an operator-only switch, optionally timed (15 min, 1 h). While a layer is off, a banner, orch status and every session's start digest say so, and logging continues (D-11, SP-070).

Interactive mockup: the Spaces pane

A new sidebar pane (SP-051). The sidebar below matches the app's current panes (MainWindow.swift); Safety Net lives inside Settings and PRs inside GitHub. Where Spaces sits in the list is illustrative. Before this pane ships, SP-072 adds a minimal Spaces section to Settings and the menu bar: the four enforcement switches with their timers, the "enforcement off" banner and the directory validator. The Basic / Advanced switch in the toolbar changes what's shown. Every view shows the same applied policy, so switching modes never changes anything. Click through the tabs and the setup steps.

A session's space is fixed at its first directory. Block other jobs refuses tool calls that reach another isolated space's folders, or run gh/git against its orgs; everything else is logged. Retag moves a source's data to another space's collection (copy, verify the count, delete; no re-embedding) and keeps it there on later refreshes. Retagging into public, or out of an isolated space, asks you to confirm (SP-071, INV-15).

No feature builds retire or merge yet. To leave a job today, remove its bindings in a draft and apply it; content no rule places goes to quarantine (INV-03), where you can purge it (orch quarantine purge, SP-028).

Which spaces can a session in each row read? Click a cell to change it. Locked cells (✕) can't be enabled: nothing outside a job reads it, and the public space reads only itself (validation rules isolated_reads_isolated, private_reads_isolated, public_reads; INV-04). A job may read a private space such as personal: off by default, switchable with a warning (D-16, SP-053).

Key flows

  • First run. The Spaces pane opens in Setup. Until you apply, orch behaves exactly as it does today (one shared space; INV-10). The sidebar badge says "Not set up", not an error. Before the pane ships, Settings and the menu bar have a minimal Spaces section with the switches and the directory validator (SP-072).
  • New job. Setup → Scan finds a new org with private repos and checkouts under a new folder. It proposes "Job: keep separate", and Review shows how many chunks it would claim from quarantine or shared.
  • A conflict appears (a checkout under one job with another job's remote). Nothing there is indexed and sessions there get no space. The Overview badge links to the folder in Rules, with two actions: move the checkout, or retag its source to one space (operator-only, moves the data, no re-embedding; SP-071). The policy has no per-path exception rule, and a root of one job can't sit inside another's (INV-14).
  • Switching a layer off. Coverage or Setup → Guard, optionally for 15 min or 1 h. The Overview banner, orch status and every session's start digest name the layer and since when; it switches back by itself when the time runs out (SP-070).
  • Leaving a job. Retire and merge are later, not planned. Today: remove the job's bindings in a draft and apply it; its content goes to quarantine, and you purge it with orch quarantine purge (SP-028).
  • From the terminal. Exists today: orch spaces draft (JSON on stdin), orch spaces diff, orch spaces apply --revision N [--accept-agent-draft] [--confirm-github-org …], orch spaces why <target>, orch spaces log verify <session>. Planned: orch spaces enforce <layer> <state> [--for 15m|1h] (SP-070), orch spaces log <session> (SP-016), orch spaces retag (SP-071), orch spaces replay (SP-047), orch sessions list (SP-017), orch quarantine list|assign|purge (SP-028), orch rag relabel [--dry-run] (SP-027), orch run <space> -- claude|codex (SP-046). There is no orch spaces list. Diff, apply and enforce refuse callers that descend from a harness or from orchd. SP-055 requires a CLI entry for every pane action.
  • Command palette (⌘K). Adds "Where would this go?…", "Review space changes" and each space by name. Like everything else in the palette, it only navigates. Applying always happens in the pane. The companion strip shows the Spaces badge (SP-055).
